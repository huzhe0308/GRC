# China 6 vs China 7 Emission Standards

## What is being compared

The current China 6 emission standard (GB 18352.6-2016) versus the upcoming China 7
(GB 18352.7-XXXX, currently in draft). China 6 has been in force since 2020 and represents
a major step from China 5, introducing WLTP-based testing and RDE requirements. China 7 is
currently under development and is expected to align with European EU7 developments, with
stricter pollutant limits and enhanced testing procedures.

The comparison draws on two key forecast documents: the VW Group EU7 Regulation Forecast
V4.0 (December 2025) and the TRRC C7 Emission Premise V6.1 (June 2026), which establish
the industry position on expected C7 requirements.

## Analysis

### China 6 Context (Current State)

China 6 (GB 18352.6-2016) was a major regulatory step, implementing WLTP-based laboratory
testing and Real Driving Emissions (RDE) testing for the first time in China. The standard
introduced limits for NOx, PM, PN (particle number), CO, HC, and NMHC. Implementation was
phased: new type approvals from July 2020, all vehicles from July 2023. The standard also
expanded OBD requirements beyond China 5 levels.

^[raw/papers/OBD_2_GB+18352.6-2016_C6.md]

### China 7 Expected Changes (Based on Forecasts)

Based on the EU7 Regulation Forecast V4.0 and TRRC C7 Emission Premise V6.1, the following
changes are expected for China 7:

1. **Stricter Pollutant Limits:** C7 will reduce NOx, PM, and PN limits further, potentially
   aligning with EU7 levels. The EU7 forecast indicates NOx limits may drop to 30-40 mg/km
   for gasoline vehicles.

2. **Enhanced RDE Requirements:** More stringent real-driving emission test conditions,
   including expanded temperature ranges, extended urban driving proportions, and tighter
   conformity factors. RDE may also include new pollutant monitoring (e.g., NH3, N2O).

3. **Extended OBD Monitoring:** More comprehensive on-board diagnostic monitoring covering
   additional components and lower threshold values. Expected alignment with EU7 OBD
   requirements including new monitor types.

4. **New Test Scenarios:** Potentially includes new driving scenarios for RDE, extended
   cold-start emission testing, and enhanced evaporative emission controls.

5. **PHEV/EV Testing Updates:** Updated test procedures for plug-in hybrids and electric
   vehicles, including range testing and charge-sustaining mode emissions.

6. **Implementation Timeline:** Aligned with EU7 timeline (expected ~2027-2028), with a
   transition period for existing type approvals.

### EU7 Alignment Context

The EU7 Regulation Forecast V4.0 (December 2025) from VW Group provides the group-wide
forecast for EU7 regulatory requirements for M1 and N1 vehicles. Key EU7 developments
driving C7 alignment include:
- Stricter NOx and PN limits
- Extended RDE testing scope (more conditions, lower conformity factors)
- Enhanced OBD with new monitor types
- New pollutant regulations (NH3, N2O, formaldehyde)
- Updated evaporative emission requirements

^[raw/articles/QA_12_EU7_Regulation_Forecast_V4.0.md]

## Verdict

China 7 represents a significant evolution from China 6, driven by alignment with European
EU7 developments. The TRRC has confirmed the C7 Emission Premise V6.1 in June 2026,
establishing the groupwide position on C7 emission legislation. VW Group has published an
EU7 Regulation Forecast V4.0 detailing expected requirements.

OEMs should prepare for:
- Stricter pollutant limits requiring advanced aftertreatment systems
- Enhanced RDE testing with tighter conformity factors
- Extended OBD monitoring requirements
- New test scenarios requiring updated test facilities and procedures
- Timeline alignment with EU7 (expected ~2027-2028 implementation)

The transition from China 6 to China 7 is comparable in scope to the China 5 → China 6
transition, requiring significant engineering investment in emission control systems,
testing capabilities, and OBD development.
