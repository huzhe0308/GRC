Performance Requirement and Testing Method for Lane Keeping
Assist (LKA)System of Passenger Cars
1
The Standard specifies the test conditions and test methods required for LKA of the
passenger car.
The Standard is applicable to the vehicles installed with LKA, and can be referred to by
other vehicles.
2
Normative References
The following documents are essential for the application of this Document. For dated
references, only the dated edition applies to this document.

Terms and Definitions
The following terms and definitions defined in GB 5768.3 and GB/T 26773-2011 are
applicable to the Document. For the convenience of use, some terms and definitions in GB/T
26773–2011 are listed below repeatedly.3.1
Driving area in which the driver does not need to change the driving route and there is no

Visible road traffic markings used to determine lane boundary.
3.3lane departure
The outer edge of front wheels goes beyond or is about to go beyond the side line of the
lane.3.4rate of departure
The vertical component of the speed at which the vehicle approaches the lane boundary
3.5lane keeping assist (LKA)
LKA refers to real-time monitoring on the relative position between the vehicle and the
lane side line during running of the vehicle, with continuous control over transverse motion of
the vehicle or that when necessary, to keep the lane within the original lane.

3.5.1lane departure prevention
Lane departure prevention refers to real-time monitoring on the relative position between
the vehicle and the lane side line during running of the vehicle, to control transverse motion of
the vehicle when vehicle departure is about to occur, so as to help the driver keep the vehicle
within the original lane.3.5.2lane centering control
Lane centering control refers to real-time monitoring on the relative position between the
vehicle and the lane side line, to realize continuous automatic control over transverse motion
of the vehicle, to keep the vehicle in the center of the lane.
4
Requirements4.1 General requirements4.1.1
The system should be able to identify the relative position between the vehicle and
the lane side line under a favorable lane side line environment, to help the driver keep the
vehicle within the original lane.4.1.2
The system should at least realize lane departure prevention or lane centering control.
4.1.3
The system should be capable of realizing power-on self-test on LKA-related main
electrical components and sensing elements to see whether they are under normal service.
4.1.4
The system should be designed with manual ON/OFF, and avoid misoperation by
the driver.4.1.5
The system should monitor its own state and provide clear and intelligible state
information for the driver, including system failure, system standby/activation and system
ON/OFF etc. The system ON/OFF information can be viewed through indirect methods such
as menu call.4.1.6
Function suppression, failure and quit conditions should be set for the system and be
illustrated through the instructions of the motor vehicle products.
4.1.7See Annex A for state switching of the system.4.1.8
The system’s requirements for functional safety should be formulated as per GB/T
34590 (all parts) and comply with the requirements in Annex B.
4.2 Performance requirement4.2.1
Such function as lane departure prevention should guarantee that lane departure is
within 0.4m on the outer side of the lane side line; such function as lane centering control
should guarantee that lane departure is within the outer side of the lane side line.
4.2.2
The longitudinal deceleration arising from lane departure prevention should not be
more than 3m/s2, and the speed decrease should not exceed 5m/s.
4.2.3
The lateral acceleration arising from system activation should not be more than
3m/s2, and the change rate in lateral acceleration of the vehicle should not exceed 5m/s3.
4.2.470km/h~120km/h.

The requirements for test environment are as follows in addition to special provisions:
a)The visibility is more than 1km;b)
The average wind speed is not more than 3m/s; the maximum wind speed is not
more than 5m/s;c)The air temperature is -20°C~45°C;d)
The ambient light illumination should be above 500lx and be distributed in a uniform

The running direction of the vehicle should not be parallel to the direction of direct
sunlight.5.2 Requirements for test roadThe test road should comply with the following conditions:a)
The test roads should be flat, dry and have an asphalt or concrete pavement under
good conditions, and the peak friction coefficient of pavement should be more than
0.8;b)
The test road should be long enough to meet the needs for test;
c)
For setting of lane side line, the requirements in GB 5768.3 should be followed. The
lane side line should be in white or yellow, and such line should be solid or dotted
ones. The lane side line should be under good conditions, without defects (such as
damage and covering) affecting LKA sensing.
5.3 Requirements for vehicles under test
The mass of the vehicle under test should lie between the sum between the curb weight
of the whole vehicle and the total mass (total mass of the driver and the testing equipment
should not exceed 150kg) of the driver and testing equipment and the maximum total mass
allowed. The state of the vehicle under test should not be changed once test starts.
5.4 Requirements of test instruments5.4.1Test parameters
Test parameters of the instruments for data acquisition and recording of the vehicle under
test should include but not limited to the followings:
a)
Speed of the vehicle under test in the entire testing process;
b)
Departure speed of the vehicle under test in the entire testing process;
c)
Lateral acceleration of the vehicle under test in the entire testing process;
d)
Distance between the vehicle under test and the lane side line in the entire testing
process.5.4.2Measuring accuracy
The data collection and recording instrument of the vehicle under test should at least
meet the following precision requirements:
a)The speed accuracy is required to be 0.1km/h;b)The departure speed accuracy is required to be 0.05km/h;c)
The lateral acceleration accuracy is required to be 0.1m/s2;
d)
The accuracy in relative distance with the lane side line is required not more than
0.02m;

Test Method6.1 Type of testThere are three types of tests:a)Tests on straight lane departure prevention;b)
Tests on curved lane departure prevention;
c)Tests on lane centering control.
The vehicles designed with lane departure prevention should be subject to tests on
straight lane departure prevention and curved lane departure prevention, while those designed
with centering control should be subject to tests on lane centering control.
6.2 Tests on straight lane departure prevention
In the course of tests, the vehicle under test should run along a straight line within the
lane. Once the vehicle under test reaches and runs at a constant speed of (70±2)km/h, depart
the vehicle under test towards left or right at a departure speed of (0.4±0.2)m/s (Fig. 1). The
testing process should comply with the requirements in 4.2.1.
Fig. 1
Schematic Diagram for Tests on Straight Lane Departure Prevention
6.3 Tests on curved lane departure prevention
The test road includes one straight lane and one curved lane. The length of the curved
lane should guarantee that the vehicle can run for above 5s. The curved lane can be classified
into the constant-curvature part and the variable-curvature part. The curvature of the constant-
curvature part is 2×10-3 m-1 (radius: ≤500m); the variable-curvature part is the section
connecting the straight lane and the curved lane featured by constant curvature. The curvature
is increased from 0 to 2×10-3 m-1 in a linear manner with the length of the curved lane, and the
change rate in curvature dc/ds should not exceed 4×10-5 m-2 (Fig. 2).

Annex A(Informative)System state and transitionSee the figure below for system state and switchingFig. A.1 System state and transition
The relationship between system state and switching is described as follows:
a)
ON and OFF of LKA can be realized automatically or manually.
b)
If under the standby state, LKA should monitor the running state of the vehicle in a
real-time manner, but will not perform any lane keeping operation. If the running
state complies with the conditions of system activation, the system can be switched
from standby to activated automatically or after confirmation by the driver.
c)
If under the activated state, LKA should monitor the running state of the vehicle in a
real-time manner. When the quit conditions of the system are met, the system is
switched from activated to standby.d)
When LKA is activated, in case of undesired lane departure, LKA should control
transverse motion of the vehicle, to help the driver keep the vehicle within the
original lane. LKA can stop or reduce unnecessary lane keep actions as per some
pre-designed instructions in addition to above.

Annex B(Normative)Functional Safety RequirementsB.1 General
Potential hazards (such as car crash due to unexpected transverse motion during normal
running of the vehicle) will be triggered in case of abnormal functions in the electronic and
electrical system related to vehicle safety. GB/T 34590 illustrates the requirements for
functional safety which should be met by the electronic and electrical system related to
vehicle safety in the life cycle of safety, to avoid or lower the risks triggered by system failure.
The Annex specifies special requirements for files, failure strategies and confirmation
tests respecting functional safety of LKA.
The Annex not aims at the nominal performance of LKA, nor serves as the specific
guidance for development of functional safety of LKA. It specifies the methods which should
be followed during design and the information which should be provided during system
verification and confirmation, to prove that the system can realize the concept of functional
safety under both normal operation and failure state and meet all applicable performance
requirements specified in the Standard.B.2 DocumentsB.2.1Requirements
There must be corresponding documents to explain the functional concept and functional
safety concept of this system, and it is also required to meet the following requirements:
a)
Illustrate functional concept, internal/external interfaces, potential failure, risks and
safety measures of the system;b)
Demonstrate that the potential failure sources have been considered in the design of
the system, including the random hardware failure and systematic failure, and
engineering practices of relevant fields have been applied;
Case: Annex E in GB/T 34590.5—2017 gives the design practice for failure in the
random hardware.c)
In order to support the confirmation test, explain how to check the working
conditions of the system both in normal operating condition and failure mode.
B.2.2Definition of item
B.2.2.1 The functional concept of related items should be described and a functional
description list should be provided.
Note 1: In GB/T 34590.1, relevant item refers to one or a group of system
performing the functions of the whole vehicle. For instance, definitions of
relevant items of LKA can include the front camera, Electronic Control
Unit (ECU), steering control module and driver warning system etc.
Note 2: Describe the functions which can be sensed from the perspective of the
whole vehicle and detail them.
B.2.2.2 The scope of related items should be defined, the systems and elements in related
items should be clearly defined, and the external systems or elements that have interactive
relations with the related items should be identified.
B.2.2.3 The operating conditions and constraints of related items must be defined, and the
limits of effective scope of working must be explained for corresponding system functions.

identification mark must be changed accordingly.
B.2.3Hazard analysis and risk assessment (HARA)
B.2.3.1 Functional faults of related items must be analyzed and classified.
Example: Typical analytical methods, such as Hazard and Operability Analysis
(HAZOP).
B.2.3.2 The list of potential hazards should be given as per the target scenario and target
users of the vehicle, and corresponding Automotive Safety Integrity Level (ASIL) should be
defined. See GB/T 34590.3.
B.2.3.3 Safety goals should be defined and classified for potential hazards.
B.2.4Functional safety concept
B.2.4.1 Safety measures (including external measures) adopted during the design and used to
satisfy relevant safety goals in the event of system failure should be described. The following
safety measures can be taken:a)
Use part of the system to maintain working. If an operation mode is selected to
maintain part of the performance when a specific failure occurs, the conditions must
be explained and the effect must be defined.
b)
Switch to a separate backup system. If the backup system is selected to achieve the
safety goal, the principle of the switching mechanism, the redundancy logic and
level and the check features of the backup system must be explained, and the effect
of the backup system must be defined.c)
Enter a safe state by closing the upper layer function If the upper layer function is
selected to be closed, all corresponding output control signals related to the function
must be prohibited to limit the propagation of interference.
d)
Reduce the risk exposure time to an acceptable time interval through the driver’s
warning.
B.2.4.2 In case of failure in LKA, the driver should be warned through warning signal or
prompt message.
B.2.4.3 The software structure of the system should be explained with the note of design
methods and tools applied.B.2.5Safety analysis

B.2.6Confirmation test
The following tests must be carried out in accordance with the description of relevant
documents in B.2 to confirm the concept of the system and the functional safety concept:
a)Confirm the functional concept of the system
Unless it is necessary to carry out a functional test in accordance with the special test
procedures specified in the Standard or other standards, the functional test under the
non-fault state of the vehicle system must be carried out in accordance with the
functional concept of A.2.2.1, which is used as a method to determine the normal
operating level of the system.b)Confirm the functional safety concept of the system
According to the functional safety concept of B.2.4, the effect of internal faults of
components must be simulated by applying corresponding output signals to the electrical and
electronic components or mechanical components of the system in order to check the response
of the system when an individual component fails.
The confirmation result should be consistent with the conclusion of functional safety
concept, and explain the adequacy of relevant safety concept and its implementation effects.
