# UN R79 Steering Equipment Regulation

## Overview

UN Regulation No. 79 establishes uniform provisions for the approval of steering equipment in M, N, and O category vehicles. The regulation covers both conventional and advanced steering systems, ensuring safety, reliability, and performance standards. It defines requirements for new technologies including Advanced Driver Assistance Steering Systems (ADAS) and corrective steering functions, with provisions for mechanical connection requirements, steering effort limits, failure management, and lane-keeping assistance.

## Scope and Requirements

- **Scope**: Applies to steering equipment of vehicles of Categories M, N, and O.
- **Exclusions**: Does not apply to steering equipment with purely pneumatic transmission; Autonomous Steering Systems as defined in paragraph 2.3.3.; vehicles of M/N categories without manual steering controls for normal operation.
- **Mechanical Connection**: Traditionally requires a positive mechanical link between steering control (steering wheel) and road wheels; advances in technology allow elimination of mechanical steering column with alternative safety measures.
- **System Types**: Covers conventional steering, Advanced Driver Assistance Steering Systems (ADAS), Corrective Steering Functions, and Automatically Commanded Steering Functions (ACSF).
- **Performance Requirements**: Sets limits on steering effort, response times, and failure management to ensure steering remains operational even during partial system failure.
- **Lane-Keeping**: Defines performance criteria for lane-keeping assistance, automated lane changes, and emergency steering functions.
- **Emergency Steering**: System shall implement strategies to draw external attention (emergency call, horn, hazard lights) when driver remains unresponsive after RMF brings vehicle to standstill.
- **Supplement 9 Changes**: Clarifies the version of UN-R 171 that vehicles with ACSF must comply with, depending on ACSF category.
- **Testing**: Includes static and dynamic assessments of steering forces, failure simulations, and performance evaluations under real-world driving conditions.
