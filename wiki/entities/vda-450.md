# VDA 450 Recommendation — Electrical Power Supply for Automated Driving

## Overview

VDA Recommendation 450 (April 2023) provides comprehensive guidance on the electrical power supply system (Energiebordnetz, EBN) for automated driving in the context of ISO 26262:2018. Published by the German Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA), this recommendation addresses the critical challenge that automated driving functions (SAE J3016 levels 3-5) require Fail-Active power supply — unlike traditional Fail-Passive functions, the power supply must continue operating after a fault to avert danger and reach a safe state. The recommendation defines safety concepts, timing requirements, ASIL decomposition strategies, and emergency operation procedures for the EBN, serving as a de facto industry standard for electrical power supply architecture in highly automated vehicles.

## Scope and Requirements

- **Scope**: Addresses the electrical power supply system (EBN) in the context of automated driving (SAE level 3-5), defining requirements for power supply channels, safety concepts, and emergency operations. Transfer to lower SAE levels is permissible and useful.
- **Also Applicable To**: Safety-relevant systems without mechanical/hydraulic/pneumatic fallback levels, such as Brake-by-Wire and Steer-by-Wire.
- **Exclusions**: Communication networks (bus systems, Ethernet, PWM), intrinsic protection (wire protection, arc protection, high-voltage safety).
- **Safety Requirements Aggregation** (§4.1.2): Aggregates safety requirements of the SR-Vehicle-Function ADS derived from technical rules, regulations, and standards — covering steering, braking, lighting/visibility, and ADS functions (Annexes A1-A4).
- **ISO 26262 Application** (§4.2):
  - **Timing Requirements** (§4.2.1): FTTI (Fault Tolerant Time Interval), FHTI (Fault Handling Time Interval), FHTTI (Fault Handling Time Interval for the EBN), MPFDTI (Multiple Point Fault Detection Time Interval), MPFHTTI
  - **Independence** (§4.2.2): Independence between EBN channels and within decomposed sources in an EBN channel; use of homogeneous redundancy
  - **Freedom from Interference** (§4.2.3): Between EBN channels, for sufficiently independent sources, and within an EBN channel
  - **Emergency Operation** (§4.2.4): Strategies for reaching safe state; integrity during emergency operation; timing of transition; temporal restrictions (EOTTI); operating/control concept; return to nominal function
  - **Predictable Misuse** (§4.2.5): Assessment of foreseeable misuse scenarios
- **EBN Elements** (§6): Defines six element types with functional requirements:
  - **Active Sources** (§6.1): Types include generators, DC/DC converters, batteries; requirements from availability and freedom from interference
  - **Passive Sources** (§6.2): Energy storage elements; availability and interference requirements
  - **Active Separating/Connecting Elements** (§6.3): Switches, relays, fuses with active control
  - **Passive Separating/Connecting Elements** (§6.4): Fuses, diodes without active control
  - **QM and SR Loads** (§6.5): Quality management and safety-related loads
  - **Energy Management** (§6.6): Power distribution and management functions
- **EBN Classification** (§5.2): EBN as an Item (standalone safety-relevant system) or as a Subsystem of one or more Items — with comparison of features for both approaches.
- **Budgeting** (§5.5): Determination of target values for failure rates and metrics (PMHF, SPFM, LFM) per ISO 26262 Part 5.

## Technical Details

- **Fail-Active Concept**: For SAE level 3-5, Fail-Passive is insufficient — vehicle operation must continue after a fault to avert danger and reach safe state. Power supply must be safety-relevant.
- **EBN Nomenclature**: Terminal designations (e.g., Kl30_s1_B(D)) encoding source, channel, and decomposition information (Table 1).
- **Topologies** (Annex C): 5 topology variants presented with characteristics of central elements (Figures 10-14).
- **Case Studies** (Annex D): Two detailed case studies with conceptual architecture topologies showing safety-relevant EBN design.
- **Quantitative Metrics** (Annex E): Derivation of PMHF, SPFM, LFM target metrics per ISO 26262 Part 5, with scenario-based calculations (Figures 17-18). PMHF budget calculation dependent on diagnostic coverage (DC_Rück) shown in Table 8.
- **Timing Derivation** (Annex F): FTTI/FHTTI derivation using steering as example (Figure 19); FHTTI/MPFHTTI derivation for EBN (Figure 20).
- **Emergency Operation Temporal Restriction (EOTTI)**: Defines maximum duration for emergency operation before safe state must be reached; operating concept may allow restricted driving with warnings to avoid secondary hazards (e.g., emergency stops in dangerous locations).
- **Safety Goal Allocation**: Example allocation of safety goals and requirements to EBN elements and components at channel level (Tables 3-4).
- **Normative References** (Annex A): Detailed requirements from steering (A1), braking (A2), lighting/visibility (A3), and ADS (A4) functions arising from technical rules, regulations, and standards.
