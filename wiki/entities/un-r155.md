# UN R155 Cybersecurity Management System

## Overview

UN Regulation No. 155 establishes uniform provisions for the approval of vehicles with regard to cybersecurity and the Cyber Security Management System (CSMS). The regulation requires vehicle manufacturers to implement a systematic, risk-based approach to identify and manage cybersecurity threats throughout the vehicle lifecycle — from development through production and post-production phases. It applies to vehicles of Categories L, M, N, and O equipped with at least one electronic control unit, requiring both a certified CSMS and type approval demonstrating that cybersecurity risks are adequately managed. UN R155 has become the global benchmark for automotive cybersecurity regulation, adopted by UNECE contracting parties including the EU, Japan, Korea, and others.

## Scope and Requirements

- **Scope**: Applies to vehicles of Categories L, M, N, and O with regard to cybersecurity, if fitted with at least one electronic control unit (ECU).
- **CSMS Definition** (§2.3): A systematic risk-based approach defining organizational processes, responsibilities, and governance to treat risk associated with cyber threats to vehicles and protect them from cyber-attacks.
- **Key Definitions**: Vehicle type (essential E/E architecture and external interfaces), cyber security, CSMS, development phase, production phase, post-production phase, mitigation, risk, risk assessment, risk management, threat, vulnerability.
- **Manufacturer Obligations** (§5.1.1): Manufacturer shall demonstrate through document checks that they have:
  - (a) Collected and verified information through the supply chain to identify and manage supplier-related risks
  - (b) Documented risk assessments (conducted during development or retrospectively), test results, and mitigations applied to the vehicle type, including design information supporting the risk assessment
  - (c) Implemented appropriate cybersecurity measures in the design of the vehicle type
  - (d) Capability to detect and respond to possible cybersecurity attacks
  - (e) Logging of data to support detection and forensic analysis of attacks
- **Testing Verification** (§5.1.2): Approval Authority verifies by testing a vehicle of the type that the manufacturer has implemented documented cybersecurity measures; testing focused on high-risk areas from risk assessment.
- **Refusal Grounds** (§5.1.3): Approval refused if manufacturer did not perform exhaustive risk assessment (§7.3.3), did not consider all Annex 5 Part A threats, did not implement proportionate mitigations, did not secure aftermarket software environments, or did not perform sufficient pre-approval testing.
- **CSMS Certification** (§6): Requires Certificate of Compliance for CSMS (Annex 4) with model Declaration of Compliance (Annex 1, Appendix 1).
- **Threat Mitigation** (Annex 5): Provides comprehensive list of threats and corresponding mitigations that manufacturers must address in their risk assessment.
- **Material Retention**: Documentation available for ≥10 years from definitive production discontinuation; additional inspection material retained for ≥10 years.
- **Conformity of Production** (§9): Ongoing compliance verification throughout production.
- **Non-Prejudice**: Does not override other UN Regulations or national/regional legislation on authorized access, privacy, or replacement parts cybersecurity.

## Technical Details

- **Approval Mark**: Circle with "E" + country code + "R155" + approval number; placed on/near vehicle data plate; must be legible and indelible.
- **Competency Requirements** (§5.3.1): Approval Authority and Technical Services must have (a) competent personnel with cybersecurity skills and automotive risk assessment knowledge; (b) implemented procedures for uniform evaluation.
- **Information Sharing** (§5.3.2): Contracting Parties notify other Approval Authorities about their assessment methods/criteria before first approval and upon method updates — for best practice collection and convergent application.
- **Supply Chain**: Manufacturer must collect and verify cybersecurity information through the supply chain, demonstrating supplier-related risks are identified and managed.
- **Post-Production Phase**: Begins when vehicle type no longer produced; ends when no operational vehicles of that type remain; manufacturer must maintain cybersecurity support throughout.
